Research
& Open Science/

My research sits at the intersection of geoinformatics, spatial epidemiology, and open science. As a Student Assistant in the Spatio-Temporal Modelling Lab of Prof. Edzer Pebesma at the Institute for Geoinformatics, University of Münster, I build Bayesian spatio-temporal models that link human mobility to infectious disease risk. As Reproducibility Manager for Replication Research and a CODECHECK reviewer, I verified the computational reproducibility of scientific papers. Below are highlights from my research journey.

research areas/
Spatial Epidemiology & Mobility
Bayesian disease mapping with R-INLA, fine-scale COVID-19 risk estimation, origin–destination mobility from mobile-phone data, and mobility-attributable risk.
Geospatial Analysis
Spatial statistics, spatio-temporal data analysis, change of support, raster/vector processing, Earth observation, and GIS for health and environmental research.
Machine Learning & AI
NLP and sentiment analysis, LLM-based question answering for geospatial catalogues, computer vision, and explainable AI.
Open Science
Computational reproducibility checking (CODECHECK), replication research, FAIR data principles, and open-source research tools.
publications/
Bayesian Spatiotemporal Attribution of COVID-19 Risk to Human Mobility Across Spatial Resolutions
Prince Oppong Boakye, Poshan Niraula & Edzer Pebesma • Manuscript under revision, International Journal of Health Geographics
Bayesian spatio-temporal models link COVID-19 cases at 100 m grid cells in New York City and Utah counties to visit activity and origin–destination infection pressure, estimating the Proportion of Cases Attributable to Mobility (PCAtM) in-sample and in held-out areas.
Code on GitHub →
Simulating Fine-Scale Population Flow for Infectious Disease Transmission Modelling
Prince Oppong Boakye (2026) • MSc Thesis, University of Münster (supervised by Prof. Edzer Pebesma and Poshan Niraula)
Fine-scale Bayesian spatio-temporal model of COVID-19 transmission risk fitted with INLA, with an origin–destination infection-pressure covariate from anonymised mobile-phone data, six model variants across three pandemic waves, and counterfactual decomposition of mobility-attributable cases.
