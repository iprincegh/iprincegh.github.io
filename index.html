about me/

Prince Oppong Boakye is a Geoinformatics and Spatial Data Science researcher and Student Assistant in the Spatio-Temporal Modelling Lab at the Institute for Geoinformatics, University of Münster. His research applies spatial statistics, Bayesian modelling, GIS and machine learning to understand disease dynamics, human mobility and environmental systems. He builds software and scalable analysis workflows that turn complex spatial data into evidence for public health and environmental decisions, and he is committed to reproducible research and open science.

how I work/
  • I start from a clear question and combine spatial statistics, GIS and software engineering to answer it with data across space and time.
  • I choose models that fit the question and the data, and I check how well their results hold up before drawing conclusions.
  • Every analysis comes with its code, data and software environment, so others can reproduce it and build on it.
  • I work across disciplines and share results in plain language through maps, dashboards, open data and writing.

about my work/
6
years

In the Spatio-Temporal Modelling Lab at ifgi, I study how human mobility shapes where infectious diseases spread. I build Bayesian spatio-temporal models in R-INLA that link mobile-phone mobility to COVID-19 cases, from 100 m grid cells in New York City to counties in Utah, and estimate the share of cases attributable to mobility.

6
years

I am now extending this work to Catalunya and Madrid, preparing open daily data on cases, mobility and covariates for their basic health areas. Alongside, I build the R tools these models need, such as nbstitch for repairing neighbourhood graphs, and I keep every analysis reproducible, with code and data others can verify and build on.
